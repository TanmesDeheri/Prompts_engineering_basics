# Branch A — E-Commerce & Logistics Overhaul

### Proposed actions

1. Optimize checkout and customer journey.
2. Implement automated, centralized inventory management.
3. Integrate inventory across warehouses, stores, and e-commerce.
4. Move fulfillment/shipping to a capable 3PL.
5. Improve delivery speed and visibility.

### 1. Margin Impact: **High**

This branch attacks several sources of margin pressure simultaneously.

Apex currently has:

* Excess inventory → high carrying costs.
* Manual tracking → inefficient inventory management.
* 5–7 day shipping → potentially higher fulfillment friction.
* 82% cart abandonment → substantial lost revenue.
* 1.2% conversion → weak monetization of existing online traffic.

Reducing excess inventory can lower carrying costs, while better inventory allocation can improve sell-through. Faster fulfillment and a better checkout experience can potentially increase online revenue without requiring a proportional expansion of the physical footprint.

### 2. CapEx: **Medium**

Likely investments include:

* Inventory-management technology.
* E-commerce/checkout improvements.
* Systems integration.
* Warehouse process changes.
* 3PL onboarding/integration.
* Data and analytics infrastructure.

The investment is meaningful, but much of it can be implemented incrementally rather than through a large physical expansion.

### 3. Execution Risk: **Medium**

The major risks are integration and organizational execution:

**E-commerce → inventory system → warehouse → 3PL → customer**

All components need to work together.

A poorly executed technology implementation could temporarily create inventory inaccuracies or fulfillment disruptions. However, the transformation can be phased, reducing the risk of a "big bang" implementation.

### **Branch A Score: 8.3/10**

**Strategic rationale:** It directly addresses the root-cause bottleneck while simultaneously attacking the e-commerce symptoms.

# Branch B — Retail Footprint Downsizing

### Proposed actions

* Close the 15 lowest-performing stores.
* Consolidate their inventory.
* Reduce associated operating costs.
* Reinvest released capital into higher-performing stores and digital operations.

This would reduce Apex's footprint from **45 to 30 stores**, a reduction of approximately **33%**.

### 1. Margin Impact: **Medium–High**

Closing genuinely underperforming locations could reduce:

* Rent.
* Store labor.
* Utilities.
* Store-level inventory requirements.
* Other fixed operating costs.

It could therefore improve margins relatively quickly.

However, store closures also eliminate potential revenue. The actual financial outcome would depend heavily on the profitability of those 15 locations rather than simply their sales volume.

### 2. CapEx: **Medium**

Store closures aren't necessarily a zero-cost strategy.

Potential costs include:

* Lease termination costs.
* Severance.
* Store liquidation/markdowns.
* Moving inventory.
* Logistics changes.
* Reconfiguration of remaining stores/warehouses.

The initial restructuring could therefore require significant cash before savings are realized.

### 3. Execution Risk: **High**

The biggest risk is strategic rather than technological.

Apex could reduce costs but simultaneously:

* Lose customers who shop physically.
* Reduce geographic coverage.
* Damage local brand presence.
* Create excess inventory during closures.
* Transfer demand to competitors.

The 25% decline in foot traffic is a warning signal, but it does not by itself prove that 15 stores should be closed. Store-level profitability and contribution margins would need to be analyzed first.

### **Branch B Score: 6.8/10**

**Strategic rationale:** Potentially powerful for cost reduction, but it addresses the symptom—an oversized/underperforming physical footprint—more directly than the underlying inventory-management problem.

# Branch C — Hybrid B2B Licensing

### Proposed actions

* Franchise selected physical stores.
* License Apex's brand.
* Shift corporate resources toward online wholesale/B2B.
* Reduce corporate ownership of physical retail operations.

### 1. Margin Impact: **Potentially High**

The model could substantially change Apex's cost structure.

Instead of funding all physical-store operations itself, Apex could potentially generate revenue through:

* Franchise/licensing fees.
* Wholesale sales.
* Brand licensing.
* Online B2B relationships.

This could create a more asset-light model.

However, the benefits are highly dependent on Apex's ability to develop a successful franchise/licensing proposition and attract capable partners.

### 2. CapEx: **Low–Medium**

Compared with building or maintaining 45 corporate stores, the model could require less physical capital.

However, Apex would still need investment in:

* B2B e-commerce infrastructure.
* Wholesale sales capabilities.
* Partner/franchise systems.
* Brand governance.
* Legal agreements.
* Training and support.
* Channel management.

### 3. Execution Risk: **High**

This is the most fundamental transformation of the three options.

Apex would effectively be changing its **business model**, not simply improving its existing operation.

Risks include:

* Difficulty finding qualified franchisees.
* Brand consistency problems.
* Channel conflict.
* Wholesale margin pressure.
* Loss of direct customer relationships.
* Dependence on external operators.
* Weak B2B demand.

### **Branch C Score: 6.1/10**

**Strategic rationale:** Potentially transformative, but it introduces substantial business-model risk before Apex has demonstrated that its underlying operational issues are under control.

## Strategic Selection: Branch A

**Branch A — E-Commerce & Logistics Overhaul emerges as the strongest candidate based on the current information.**

The key reason is **problem-solution fit**.

Apex's problems form a connected chain:

**Manual inventory tracking**
→ **poor inventory visibility**
→ **overstocking**
→ **high carrying costs**
→ **margin compression**

At the same time:

**Poor inventory/fulfillment processes**
→ **5–7 day shipping**
→ **weak online customer experience**
→ **82% cart abandonment**

Branch A addresses both sides of this equation without immediately committing Apex to a fundamental reduction or transformation of its physical business.

### Recommended sequencing

Rather than treating the branches as mutually exclusive forever, a pragmatic transformation could be:

**1. Branch A first**
Automate inventory + optimize fulfillment + improve checkout.

**2. Then evaluate Branch B using store-level data**
After obtaining better inventory and profitability data, determine whether specific stores should actually be closed.

**3. Keep Branch C as a longer-term strategic option**
Test franchise/licensing and B2B wholesale economics before committing to a corporate business-model pivot.

### Executive takeaway

> **Branch A = operational transformation.**
> **Branch B = cost restructuring.**
> **Branch C = business-model transformation.**

Given Apex's stated metrics, the first priority is to **fix the operational engine before restructuring the entire business model**. Branch A therefore provides the most direct path from the identified root cause to measurable improvements in inventory costs, fulfillment, and digital sales.
